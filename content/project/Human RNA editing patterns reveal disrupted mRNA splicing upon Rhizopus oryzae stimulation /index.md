Fungal pathogens trigger well-defined transcriptomic responses, yet our understanding of the epitranscriptome during infection remains incomplete. To partially address this knowledge gap, we focused on host adenosine deaminases acting on RNA (ADAR)-mediated adenosine-to-inosine (A-to-I) RNA editing. Leveraging publicly available RNA-seq data from human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and small airway epithelial cells (HSAECs), we observed a significant increase in A-to-I hyper-editing in response to the human fungal pathogen Rhizopus oryzae, particularly in intronic regions. Such a signature was notably absent from the response to two other dangerous fungal pathogens, Aspergillus fumigatus and Candida albicans. We observed R. oryzae to trigger a specific dysregulation of host splicing genes and an increased frequency of exon inclusion/skipping and intron retention associated with splicing factors YBX1/SRSF2. R. oryzae-driven alternative splicing led to mature transcripts with lower folding free energy and higher levels of hyper-editing, indicative of more double-stranded RNA structure. Hyper-edited RNAs are known to inhibit the interferon response, consistent with a reported downregulation of pattern recognition receptors and limited type-I interferon signaling during R. oryzae infection. Exon skipping events observed in immune genes like the Nod-like receptor NLRC5, led to transcripts with a predicted premature stop codon and likely further impair immune signaling. Taken together, these results suggest that R. oryzae disrupts host RNA splicing by inducing YBX1/SRSF2, resulting in increased levels of hyper-editing and altered antifungal immunity.
