# Fist interview with Prof. Maite Huarte

## Her recent years research interest

1. understanding how lncRNAs impact processes such as DNA replication and genomic instability (2023) -> DNA damage response strongly relies on the pivotal role of p53, a key transcription factor and tumor suppressor. In response to stress, p53 orchestrates a gene expression program that enforces cell cycle arrest to allow DNA repair or triggers apoptosis when the damage is too extensive.

## i should know:

1. What cell lines does she use?
   Her lab uses a combination of human cancer cell lines, engineered cell models, and patient-derived samples. They don't rely on one single model system; instead, they choose cell lines based on the biological question.

Frequently used models include:

Lung cancer cell lines (e.g., in studies of the lncRNA ALAL-1)
Colorectal (colon) cancer cell lines, particularly for studies of oncogenic lncRNAs and p53/MYC signaling
Human leukemia cell lines, especially for enhancer and chromatin-related transcription studies
Patient tumor samples for validation of mechanistic findings

2. What kind of cancer is she working on?
   Rather than focusing on a single cancer type, the lab studies how long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) regulate gene expression across multiple cancers.

Major cancer types include:

Lung cancer (one of the lab's strongest focuses; ALAL-1 is a key example)
Colorectal 结直肠(colon) cancer
Leukemia 白血病 (enhancer transcription and gene regulation)
More generally, solid tumors where lncRNAs regulate oncogenes (e.g., MYC) or tumor suppressors (e.g., p53).

The overarching biological theme is:

Long non-coding RNAs regulate gene expression, chromatin function, genome stability, and cancer progression.
